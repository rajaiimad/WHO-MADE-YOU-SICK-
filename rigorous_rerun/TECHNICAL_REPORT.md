## 2. What I changed and why

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| **Protocol.** Replaced the single 70/15/15 split with repeated nested cohort-disjoint CV: 5 repeats {0,1,2,42,2024} × 5-fold StratifiedGroupKFold over cohorts. Within each outer-train set, one grouped split gives train (36 cohorts) and validation (12 cohorts). | Every cohort is tested once per repeat. Validation alone is used for HPO, early stopping, calibration and thresholds. The test fold is scored only once. |
| **Lock-box.** 120 fresh cohorts (seeds 20000–20119), generated by the unchanged generator. Pre-registered (`PREREGISTRATION.md`) before any model scored it, and scored exactly once. | Removes the selection bias of developing many variants on the same 60 cohorts. |
| **Oracle ceiling.** Recomputed the generator's hidden score and P(score + N(0, 0.05) > 0.30). Validated: expected and observed positives match on every corpus (e.g. 1258 vs 1272). | Shows how much headroom exists and turns the circularity concern into a measured quantity. |
| **New detector (MPNN).** Edge-conditioned message passing over the incoming co-presence edges: m_j = φ([victim, peer channels, excess-over-benign intensity, peer backbone outputs, raw edge geometry d / FOV / audio]), aggregated as sum‖max, then ρ. Permutation-invariant and defined for any cohort size. No generator coefficients and no 1/d term are hand-coded. | This is the learned graph aggregation the paper promised but did not train. A CUDA GPU is available on this machine, so the "compute-blocked" limitation no longer applies. |
| **Fair baselines.** Every family (LR, HGB, torch MLP, MPNN) is tuned with an equal grid of at most 16 configs inside each fold, selected by validation PR-AUC. The paper's MLP is also run as published. | A fair comparison, plus a direct replication of the published model. |
| **Imbalance handling.** Class weighting (`balanced` / pos_weight sqrt) is a hyperparameter selected on validation. Platt calibration is fitted on validation. | Prevalence is 1.4%. Weighting distorts probabilities, so calibration is needed. |
| **Thresholds.** F1-optimal and FPR-budget thresholds (1/5/10/20%) are chosen on validation, and the achieved test FPR is reported. | The old FNR@FPR was read off the test curve. |
| **Statistics.** Hierarchical cohort bootstrap CI (resample cohorts, recompute the pooled metric per repeat, average). Paired comparisons use the Nadeau–Bengio corrected resampled t-test plus a bootstrap CI of the difference. | The cohort is the independent unit. Folds of repeated CV are not independent. |
| **Attribution features.** Added "relative" (per-channel contrast against the other peers) and "agnostic" (channel-identity-free summaries of excess over the public benign band, plus geometry). Same HGB capacity throughout. | Tests whether the unseen-channel failure is a representation problem. |
| **Robustness.** Shift and adaptive corpora use fresh seeds and re-simulated labels. Federated rerun uses no pooled initialisation and federated sufficient-statistic standardisation. ALIE was added as an out-of-threat-model stress test. | Fixes F5 and F8. |
| **Leakage harness.** `leakage_checks.py` runs 11 automated checks; all pass (section 7). | Requirement 5. |

## 3. Preprocessing and final features

* **Per-user backbone** (frozen, on-device): StandardScaler + MLP(64,32), 4-class severity, trained on an independent calibration corpus. Outputs 4 log-probabilities plus uncertainty (1 − max p).
* **Prototype (paper) features, 20-D:** victim backbone (5) | edge-weighted mean of peer backbone (5) | edge-weighted mean of peer channels (5) | max over peers of w × excess-over-benign intensity (5). Edge weight = σ(1.5·e^(−d/3) + FOV + 0.75·audio_prox).
* **MPNN inputs.**
  * Victim: backbone outputs (5). The `mpnn_raw` variant adds 20 raw telemetry channels.
  * Per incoming edge (17): peer channels / physical max (5), excess over the benign band (5), peer backbone (5), d, FOV overlap, audio proximity (3). An optional log d is a selectable hyperparameter.
* **Standardisation:** fitted on training cohorts only (edges flattened over slots).
* **Causality:** everything is computed from frame t only; the temporal variants use frames ≤ t within the same track.
* **Attribution** per directed edge (frame t): see `run_attribution.py` docstring.

## 4. Models evaluated

Prevalence baseline, victim-only (published MLP on backbone; tuned HGB on backbone + raw), prototype × {published MLP, LR, HGB, torch MLP}, MPNN, MPNN + victim raw telemetry, and the oracle ceiling.

Ablations:
* 10 feature-source and aggregation isolations (HGB).
* 10 MPNN ablations.
* Imbalance on/off.
* Temporal windows K ∈ {1, 4, 8, 16}.

### 5.3 What contributes (T5–T7)

* **Peer channels carry the signal.** HGB on peer-channel aggregates alone gives PR 0.383, matching the full prototype (0.388). Victim, peer-backbone, geometry-only and permuted-peer inputs are all weak (ROC 0.58–0.66, PR ≤ 0.028).
* **Geometry-aware weighting helps PR, not ROC.** Edge weights give PR 0.388; uniform weights 0.248; random weights 0.237. ROC is 0.92–0.93 for all three, which confirms the paper's RQ2 statement.
* **MPNN ablations.** Only edge geometry is individually significant: removing it drops PR by 0.453 (p < 0.001). Removing peer-backbone, intensity or victim features, switching sum‖max to mean or max, and toggling pos-weight or log d each change PR by at most 0.05, none significant over 25 folds. The gain therefore comes from learning per-edge channel × geometry interactions before aggregation.
* **Adding raw victim telemetry does not help.** mpnn_raw gives PR 0.712 vs 0.760.
* **RQ3 (temporal context): no benefit.** Causal windows lower PR monotonically: K=1 0.388, K=4 0.379, K=8 0.367, K=16 0.362. This is expected, because the label depends on frame t only.
* **Imbalance.** Class weighting changes HGB PR from 0.368 to 0.388 (not significant). Every weighted model needs Platt calibration (e.g. HGB ECE 0.050 → 0.005).

Leave-one-channel-out (LOCO), mean over channels (ROC / top-1):

| features | ROC | top-1 |
|---|---|---|
| paper | 0.723 | 0.675 |
| relative | 0.735 | 0.690 |

* With agnostic features, held-out channels other than audio reach top-1 0.988–0.994. Paired over (channel, fold), agnostic beats paper features 114 of 119 times on ROC.
* **Spatial audio still fails** (ROC 0.52, top-1 0.52). It is the only channel that acts outside the field of view, so its labelling rule cannot be learned from the other channels.

### 5.5 Robustness (T10b–T10e, T11)

* **Generator shifts** (coefficients, thresholds, pulse profile, low intensity; labels re-simulated): MPNN ROC 0.939–0.996 and PR 0.54–0.78. The prototype gets 0.80–0.98 and PR 0.19–0.46.
* **Feature-level decoupling** (lock-box peer edges permuted): all models drop to chance (ROC 0.44–0.53). **Generator-level time shuffle:** MPNN still reaches ROC 0.962 / PR 0.477, because shuffling within a track keeps the attacker's channel level. This matches the paper's conclusion that the task recovers a generator-intrinsic signal.
* **Cohort size (trained on N=4).**
  * N=3: MPNN 0.979 / 0.581.
  * N=6: MPNN 0.962 / 0.610.
  * N=8: MPNN 0.935 / 0.565.
  * N=2: MPNN degrades (0.891 / 0.640) and the torch MLP prototype collapses (0.577), but mpnn_raw (0.995 / 0.803) and the prototype LR/HGB (0.97–0.98) hold.
  * Transfer to an unseen cohort size is model-dependent and must be stated as such.
* **Adaptive attacker** (intensity α swept 1 → 0.2, labels re-simulated). Harm falls from 3.5 to 0.66 unsafe frames per attacked track. At the 10%-FPR operating point, the prototype's recall collapses from 0.79 to 0.26, while MPNN keeps 0.90–0.96 and misses ≤ 0.12 unsafe frames per attacked track.
* **Federated MPNN** (48 clients, 30 rounds, 5 seeds). Clean FedAvg reaches 0.937 / 0.572, below the centralised 0.979 / 0.760 (a cost of the round budget).
  * **Gaussian poisoning:** FedAvg collapses (PR 0.022). Median holds (PR 0.554 / 0.487 at 20% / 40%).
  * **Sign-flip at 40%:** no aggregator helps (PR 0.12–0.17).
  * **Label-flip at 40%:** median is best (0.589).
  * **ALIE** (colluding and omniscient, outside the threat model): it hurts median the most (PR 0.196 at 40%).
  * Conclusion: robustness is attack-dependent, as the paper says. There is no universal Byzantine claim.
* **Latency (RQ7)**, 1 CPU thread, feature assembly included: MPNN 0.43–0.96 ms median per cohort-frame (p95 ≤ 2.1 ms) for N = 2–8. That is well inside a 60 Hz frame budget.

## 8. Limitations that remain

1. **Circularity.** Labels are generator-derived. The MPNN closes much of the gap to a ceiling defined by the same generator: on the lock-box, PR is 0.69 against 0.88. Better detection here means better recovery of the simulator's own rule, **not** evidence about human cybersickness. No human data were used.
2. **Lock-box optimism.** The lock-box shows CV PR was optimistic for every model by 0.07–0.10. Report lock-box numbers as primary.
3. **Size transfer and channels.** Cohort-size transfer to N=2 is model-dependent. Audio-channel attribution does not transfer.
4. **Federated budget.** Federated results use 30 rounds, one outer fold per repeat and 5 seeds. Sign-flip at 40% and ALIE are not defended by any tested aggregator. No formal privacy (secure aggregation / DP) is implemented.
5. **Backbone.** The backbone was re-trained under sklearn 1.6, so victim-only numbers differ slightly from the 23-Sep audit (0.706 there; 0.635 CV and 0.691 lock-box here).
6. **Not rerun.** The counterfactual-masking analysis and CoreTurn/UFFCS were not rerun. The `accel` central difference in the generator remains (no label effect).
7. **Hyperparameter selection.** Selection used validation PR-AUC on 12 validation cohorts per fold, which is noisy. Tier-2 ablations use one fixed configuration, so their effect sizes are conditional on it.

## 10. Addendum (counterfactual rerun + a generator finding that affects every result)

### 10.1 Counterfactual source masking (`run_counterfactual.py` -> `outputs/counterfactual.json`)

Setup:
* Lock-box: 17,232 victim-frames that have a true source, from 107 cohorts.
* Final models: 5 seeds.
* Masking: the peer's five channels are set to the top of the benign band.
* dP = P(original) − P(masked).
* The ORACLE dP applies the same intervention to the generator's hidden score, so it is the ground-truth effect under simulator semantics.
* The attribution model's top-1 on these frames is 0.990.

| detector | dP true source | dP predicted source | dP random non-source | paired (pred − random) [cohort-boot 95% CI] | d_z | corr. with oracle dP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MPNN | 0.098 | 0.097 | −0.013 | 0.109 [0.090, 0.127] | 0.51 | 0.93 |
| prototype (published) | 0.095 | 0.094 | −0.006 | 0.099 [0.089, 0.109] | 0.68 | 0.70 |
| oracle (ground truth) | 0.121 | 0.121 | −0.009 | 0.131 [0.107, 0.152] | 0.46 | — |

Masking the predicted source lowers the detector score far more than masking a random peer. For the MPNN, the per-frame effect tracks the true simulator effect (r = 0.93; the prototype's r = 0.70).

The old paper value (paired ΔP 0.041) is superseded.

As in the paper, the effect is concentrated in spatial audio. dP for the true source by attack channel, MPNN: audio 0.137, every other channel ≤ 0.004. The oracle shows the same pattern (audio 0.168, others ≤ 0.010). Section 10.2 explains why.

### 10.2 Generator asymmetry: spatial audio dominates the label (validity issue)

In `xrperil_multi._peer_contribution` (also in `gen_param._peer_contrib_param`):
* **Peer outside the victim's FOV:** contribution = `audio/100 / d`, i.e. weight **1.0**.
* **Peer inside the FOV:** contribution = `(0.35·ang + … + 0.10·audio) · FOV / d`, i.e. the audio weight is **0.10** and is further multiplied by FOV overlap ≤ 1.

So the same audio jitter is ≥10× more provocative when the attacker is *out of view*. The manuscript's equation states that out-of-FOV peers contribute "only the audio term". That reads as the 0.10-weighted term, not a 10× larger one.

Consequence (measured):

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| Tracks attacked through audio | 12.3% |
| Unsafe frames from audio attacks, main corpus | 93.4% |
| Unsafe frames from audio attacks, lock-box | 88.0% |
| Unsafe frames from each other attack channel | 0.6–3.5% |

Per attack channel on the lock-box (that channel's tracks plus benign tracks), ROC / PR:

| channel (unsafe frames) | prototype, published | MPNN | oracle |
|---|---|---|---|
| spatial audio (2089) | 0.942 / 0.338 | 0.989 / 0.761 | 0.996 / 0.890 |
| angular velocity (82) | 0.562 / 0.003 | 0.959 / 0.104 | 0.994 / 0.722 |
| teleport (20) | 0.481 / 0.002 | 0.950 / 0.068 | 0.991 / 0.615 |
| particle (13) | 0.500 / 0.002 | 0.940 / 0.062 | 0.990 / 0.616 |
| flicker (15) | 0.493 / 0.003 | 0.952 / 0.068 | 0.991 / 0.647 |

Interpretation:
* **The paper's prototype is at chance for 4 of the 5 attack channels.** Its headline ROC/PR comes almost entirely from out-of-FOV audio attacks.
* The MPNN detects all five channels in ROC terms. For the non-audio channels its PR is low (0.06–0.10) because positives are extremely rare (13–82 frames).
* The same asymmetry explains why audio fails leave-one-channel-out (section 5.4) and why the counterfactual effect sits in audio (10.1).

**This must be disclosed, whatever is decided.** Two options:
1. **Keep the generator as is.** Report per-channel results and state that the label is dominated by out-of-FOV audio. All current results stand.
2. **Fix the generator** (apply the 0.10 audio coefficient out of FOV too, or apply FOV consistently) and regenerate every corpus. Then rerun the whole pipeline (~6–8 h compute) to get a balanced five-channel benchmark.

### 11.5 Robustness (v2)

**Cohort size (trained on N=4, tested on N=2 and N=8)**

| model | N=2 ROC | N=8 ROC |
|---|---|---|
| MPNN | 0.996 | 0.957 |
| published prototype | 0.186 (inverted) | 0.716 |

The prototype fails to transfer: its mean-aggregate features depend on cohort size.

**Generator shifts (labels re-simulated)**
* MPNN: ROC 0.949–0.997, PR 0.51–0.66.
* Prototype: ROC 0.62–0.91, PR 0.02–0.23. It collapses under the low-intensity shift (0.617 / 0.022).

**Decoupling controls**
* Feature-level peer permutation: chance for all models (0.49–0.59).
* Generator time-shuffle: MPNN still 0.975 / 0.475, because the attacker's channel level persists within the track.

**Adaptive attacker (intensity α from 1 to 0.2, labels re-simulated)**
* Harm falls from 4.6 to 0.76 unsafe frames per attacked track.
* Published prototype: recall at 10% FPR falls from 0.68 to 0.07–0.17; it misses up to 2.6 unsafe frames per attacked track.
* MPNN: recall stays 0.93–0.97; it misses ≤ 0.13 unsafe frames per attacked track.

**Federated MPNN (48 clients, 30 rounds, 5 seeds): weaker than in v1**

| condition | FedAvg (ROC / PR) | median (ROC / PR) |
|---|---|---|
| centralised training (reference) | 0.980 / 0.617 | — |
| clean | 0.848 / 0.117 | 0.895 / 0.200 |

* **Sign-flip at 40%:** no aggregator defends (ROC ≤ 0.61).

* **Report as a limitation:** 30 rounds over non-IID cohort clients do not reach centralised quality.

**Latency (1 CPU thread, feature assembly included):** MPNN 0.32–0.35 ms median per cohort-frame (p95 ≤ 0.85 ms) for N = 2–8.

### 11.6 v1 → v2 and implications for the manuscript

| quantity | v1 (bugged generator) | v2 (fixed generator) |
|---|---|---|
| Published prototype, lock-box ROC / PR | 0.927 / 0.306 | **0.813 / 0.191** |
| MPNN, lock-box ROC / PR | 0.981 / 0.693 | **0.987 / 0.689** |
| Prototype on non-audio attacks | at chance (0.48–0.56) | weak (0.65–0.79) |
| MPNN on non-audio attacks | 0.94–0.96 | 0.986–0.992 |
| Victim-only, lock-box ROC | 0.691 | 0.590 |
| LOCO with agnostic features, mean top-1 | 0.897 | 0.929 |
| Federated, clean FedAvg PR | 0.572 | 0.117 |

**Recommendations**
1. **Use the v2 generator and v2 results in the paper.** The v1 benchmark was effectively a single-channel task (out-of-FOV audio).
2. **Report the original prototype as substantially weaker than published.** Once the benchmark is fixed it reaches lock-box ROC 0.81 / PR 0.19. The proposed MPNN is robust to the fix, and its advantage widens (ΔPR +0.45).
3. **Drop or rewrite these claims:**
   * victim-only detection at chance
   * prototype ROC 0.92
   * LOCO 0.489 as the central negative result
   * randomised geometry having no effect on attribution
   * the old adaptive-attacker result
   * the old federated numbers
   * CoreTurn
4. **Add these limitations:**
   * The generator fix includes a calibrated gain, which must be disclosed.
   * Labels remain generator-derived (MPNN PR 0.69 vs ceiling 0.84).
   * Federated training underperforms centralised training.
   * Spatial-audio attribution remains the weakest channel.
